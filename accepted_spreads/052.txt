For lodging, they have a mattress filled with straw, and a coverlet: the straw is changed, and kept in good order. If one attempts to desert, and be taken before sunset, he must wear a ring, and a chain of eighteen pounds weight; and he must pay half his future earnings, till it amounts to a zechin, to those that apprehended him.¹ If they who are condemned for five years, desert, when retaken their term again commences: and for repeated desertions, they are more severely punished, and sometimes tortured.

The chaplain must instruct the prisoners.

In the hospital there must be all proper provisions for the sick and infirm, viz. veal, mutton, rice, fine bread, broth, good wine, etc. A physician attends, and the diet and medicines must be according to his prescription. On entering, the patients have clean linen, shirts, night-caps, and clothes. And the keepers and turnkey must examine their victuals and soup, to see that they be good, and that the quantity be according to the physician’s orders.²

ROME. At the great prison at Rome, called the New Prison, at the back of which runs the Tiber, on a stone tablet over the door, is this inscription:

JUSTITIÆ ET CLEMENTIÆ
SECURIORI AC MELIORI REORUM CUSTODIÆ
NOVUM CARCEREM
INNOCENTIUS X PONT. MAX.
POSUIT
ANNO DOMINI
MDCLV.

To Justice and Clemency,
For the more secure and better custody of criminals,
Pope Innocent X erected this New Prison
In the year of our Lord
1655.
